app-build-playbook · v2.0.0

From idea to something you use, one gate at a time

Each stage ends at a gate you can check. Reviewers advise and you decide. Your standing preferences are written once, so sessions stop re-asking them. You don't need commands: say what you want. Tap a stage to see inside it.

Project size

0

Start, or adopt

"start a new app"
GateYou agree the size, the type, the teaching mode and where to begin.
Show what happens
  • Harm questions first: real people's data? money or a deadline? health? pets or children? The answers set the size.
  • Type: a phone app, a web tool, or a tool with a scheduled routine.
  • Teaching mode: teach me, explain decisions, or just ship. Asked again each build.
  • Set-up is proved before any feature: what can be tested in the session, and how a change reaches you.
  • Adopting a half-built project: understand it first, fix only what's dangerous, keep one decision log.

1–3

Plan, spec, spike

"grill me on the plan"
GateYou could run it by hand from the plan. Every rule has a test. You've seen what data really arrives.
Show what happens
  • Plan: what it's for and the rules, in plain words, usable with no app.
  • Spec: the promises it must always keep, each with a test. Unknown input is never good news.
  • Spike: copy the real field names from the source on day one. Report counts, never values.
  • Both documents are grilled before any reviewer sees them.
4

Design, before the first screen

"show me options"
GateYou lock the look. A scan test keeps builds to it.
Show what happens
  • Brief: apps you like, by element, and what you don't want from each.
  • Round 0: one mock-up page with 2–3 directions on made-up data. Pick one, or mix.
  • Lock: a style guide that settles every visible choice, so builders don't guess.
  • After that: a mock-up before any visible change, and a wording page for new words.
  • Design depth: brief, round 0, lock, checks every build.
5

Expert panel and safety audit

"run the panel"
GateEvery serious item has your decision. The audit finds nothing serious left.
Show what happens
  • 5–6 reviewers, told what you've already decided so they don't reopen it.
  • You're told which reviewers will run, and say "start".
  • The audit loop ends by fixing, by simplifying (offered at round 3), or by building a trial to try.
  • ⚠ Anything that loosens a safety rule is asked on its own, never inside "yes to all".
6

Builds (repeat)

"merge 65"
GateEvery check finds nothing serious. Nothing reaches the real project until you say "merge".
Show what happens
Brief→Mock-up if visible→Small steps→Code stops changing→Safety · Code · Design checks, side by side→Fix, re-check→Words OK→"Merge N"→One question to you↺
  • Checks are sized to the change. Words only: a wording page. Looks only: a design check. A stricter rule: one narrow audit. Only a loosening gets the full loop.
  • Rule changes also get a logic review: do the rules still add up over weeks of use?
  • Several builds at once: one coordinator session you talk to; it briefs, relays and merges.
  • Every message ends: on main · on a branch · on your phone.
7

Release

"is that done?"
GateYou've tried it for real. You could keep it going without a session.
Show what happens
  • A tick-box checklist on your phone: where, what to tap, what you should see. Machines check what machines can.
  • Your screenshots (light, dark, largest text) find what code review can't.
  • Three days of real use before any polish.
  • An owner's manual, a renewals table, and a page of promises with what proves each.
8

Live

"log it"
LoopUse it, log ideas, build the small ones now, ship small releases.
Show what happens
  • Ideas: one list. "Log it" adds a line and carries on. No hard freeze.
  • What's missing? A whole-project sweep with an "if we skip it" table.
  • Operate: for a live tool. You say what changed; a three-line summary; one OK; then it's written and read back.
  • Routines: the routine works nothing out itself, runs only code that has been checked, and can't change without you.
  • Options: research, 3–5 ways compared, a recommendation, your doubt answered.

How a question round looks

Reply "yes to all", or "yes to all except 1: a". Your answers are read back before anything happens. Looks are never asked in words: you get a mock-up.
